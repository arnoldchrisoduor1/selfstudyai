Verified for Distributed Systems

Raft relies on a strict majority quorum ((n/2) + 1 nodes) to guarantee cluster safety and consistency during leader election and log replication. Because any two majorities of size n share at least one node in common (the pigeonhole principle) [1], it is mathematically impossible for two disjoint network partitions to both elect a separate leader simultaneously, completely preventing split-brain conditions [2].

Furthermore, because a newly elected leader must receive votes from a majority, its quorum is guaranteed to contain at least one node holding all committed entries from previous terms, ensuring leader completeness [3].
